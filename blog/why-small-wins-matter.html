Why Small Wins Matter More Than Big Goals

By Health Camper · October 2026 · 5 min read

Big goals are exciting to set and exhausting to carry. "Get fit." "Fix my sleep." "Be less stressed." They're so far away that most days it feels like you've made no progress at all, and that feeling quietly drains your motivation.

Small wins work the other way round.

What the research says

Harvard researchers Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer studied thousands of daily diary entries from people at work. Their finding, which they called the progress principle: of everything that lifted people's mood and motivation on a given day, making progress on meaningful work, even small progress, mattered most (Harvard Business Review, "The Power of Small Wins", 2011).

Progress you can see feeds motivation. Motivation makes the next bit of progress easier. It's a loop, and it can start very small.

How to use small wins

Shrink the step until it's almost silly. Not "meditate for 20 minutes", but "sit and breathe for one minute". Not "fix my sleep", but "phone on the charger by 10:30".

Make the win visible. Tick a box. Keep a streak. Write one line in a journal. Your brain needs to see that it moved.

Stack them. One win in the morning, one at midday, one at night. Three small camps a day add up to a mountain over a month.

Forgive the missed days. A streak is a tool, not a judge. Missing one day doesn't erase the climb. Just start again from the camp you're at.

"Small steps still move you up the mountain."

Everyday wellbeing writing, not medical advice. If you're struggling most days, or thinking about harming yourself, please reach out to a doctor, a local helpline, or your local emergency number.

Three small wins a day

Health Camper turns small wins into a daily climb: Camp One is a kind affirmation, Camp Two is one small goal you choose, Camp Three is a few quiet minutes of breathing or meditation. Reach all three and you plant your flag at the summit.
